% 1. HIGHER-ORDER GOAL
% ============================================================
\section{Higher-order goal\secann{...secondary framing}}

This project aims to make hostel dining fairer and more resource-efficient by
giving students and mess operators a shared, real-time signal of who is
actually eating. While the broader goal is campus resource efficiency and
financial fairness for students, the immediate engineering objective is to
translate \textit{consumption-aware billing and demand planning} into a
measurable, deployable software platform.

% ============================================================
% 2. TIME-TO-VALUE
% ============================================================
\section{Time-to-value\secann{...primary heuristic}}

\begin{itemize}
    \item We will deliver meaningful increments quickly by shipping
    consumption tracking and billing transparency as a working pilot in a
    single hostel mess before building the admin and analytics layers.
    \item We will prioritize fast feedback over completeness by validating
    each phase's release criteria (e.g., billing view matches manual
    reconciliation) before starting the next phase.
    \item Success is defined by what can be delivered and measured in the
    first phase --- reliable opt-in and opt-out and accurate billing --- then
    improved based on pilot feedback.
\end{itemize}

% ============================================================
% 3. PROBLEM STATEMENT
% ============================================================
\section{Problem Statement}

Students in hostels are charged a fixed mess fee regardless of how many meals
they actually eat, while mess operators plan food production from estimated
rather than confirmed demand. Common pain points include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fixed billing, variable usage:} students who travel, eat
    out, or skip meals still pay the same flat amount.
    \item \textbf{Low transparency:} students cannot easily see what they are
    being charged for versus what they actually consumed.
    \item \textbf{Demand guesswork:} the mess prepares food against estimated
    or historical turnout rather than confirmed participation.
    \item \textbf{Manual reconciliation:} consumption and billing are tracked
    via registers, WhatsApp, or spreadsheets, forcing rework and disputes.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Why this matters now (contextual relevance):} With rising mess costs
and limited administrative bandwidth, even a small improvement in consumption
visibility can noticeably improve billing fairness and reduce food waste.

% ============================================================
% 4. PROPOSED SOLUTION
% ============================================================
\section{Proposed Solution\secann{...Software Proposition}}

\subsection{Overview}

Build a web-based ``Consumption-to-Bill'' platform with the following
components:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Meal participation and opt-out:} students mark or opt out of
    upcoming meals before a configured cutoff.
    \item \textbf{Consumption-based billing engine:} eligible meals consumed
    are converted into transparent, itemized charges.
    \item \textbf{Menu module:} students view published menus.
    \item \textbf{Feedback module:} students submit ratings and complaints.
    \item \textbf{Admin/ops dashboard:} billing summaries, participation
    trends, and backlog of complaints.
    \item \textbf{Role-based access control:} student versus administrator
    permissions on billing and consumption data.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Core workflow}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Student views the published menu and marks participation (or opts
    out) before the cutoff.
    \item System records eligible meal participation and locks it at the
    cutoff.
    \item Meal is served; consumption is logged against the participation
    record.
    \item Billing engine converts eligible consumption into itemized charges
    at the configured meal rate.
    \item Student views updated balance and billing breakdown; admin views
    demand and reconciliation dashboards.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational constraints for a campus/hostel setting}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep the solution usable on low-to-mid range devices via
    mobile-first, responsive web design, given meal-time usage patterns.
    \item Avoid dependence on advanced research algorithms; focus on
    workflow, billing correctness, and automation.
    \item Keep the billing model configurable per institution, since colleges
    differ in mess-fee policy.
    \item Design for deployability using common web hosting and managed
    databases.
\end{itemize}

% 5. SOLUTION APPROACH
% ============================================================
\section{Solution Approach\secann{...Engineering focus}}

This is an engineering project, so we focus on scalable billing and workflow
aspects rather than novel algorithm research.

\subsection{Billing/consumption assistance\secann{...non-research}}

Eligibility and anomaly checks will be heuristic-based:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Apply simple, rule-based cutoff and eligibility checks (e.g., no
    opt-out after the configured window) rather than predictive models.
    \item Flag unusual participation patterns (e.g., repeated late opt-outs)
    using basic thresholds, without claiming state-of-the-art detection.
    \item Show flagged items to staff for review; never auto-charge or
    auto-waive a disputed record.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Web architecture\secann{...mobile-responsive solution}}

A typical 3-tier web architecture:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Frontend:} mobile-responsive UI for students and staff
    (menu, participation, billing, dashboard).
    \item \textbf{Backend API:} authentication, participation/opt-out,
    billing calculation, menu and feedback endpoints.
    \item \textbf{Database:} store users, meals, participation records,
    billing history, and feedback.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{CI/CD and fast delivery enabler}

CI/CD will be used to:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Automatically build and run tests on every change.
    \item Produce repeatable deployment artifacts to staging.
    \item Enable safe and frequent releases of incremental features.
\end{itemize}

% 6. EVALUATION CRITERION
% ============================================================
\section{Evaluation Criterion\secann{...measurable and attributable}}

We define success using measurable metrics tied to the core workflow.

\subsection{Primary evaluation metric}

\textbf{Consumption-to-Billing Latency (CBL):} time between a meal window
closing and the corresponding charge appearing in the student's billing
ledger.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Target: median CBL less than or equal to 24 hours during the pilot window.
    \item Attribution: measured from database timestamps (meal-window close
    versus billing ledger update).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Secondary evaluation metrics}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Forecast error reduction:} variance between expected and
    actual meal participation, compared to the pre-SmartMess baseline.
    \item \textbf{Billing dispute rate:} percentage of billing cycles with a
    student-raised dispute.
    \item \textbf{User clarity:} student-reported clarity of billing using a
    simple 1--5 feedback question.
    \item \textbf{Reliability:} availability of the platform for login,
    participation, and billing views during meal hours (e.g., greater than or
    equal to 99 percent uptime in pilot).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pilot validation plan\secann{...high-level}}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Recruit 10--20 pilot students and 3--5 mess-staff roles in a single
    hostel.
    \item Compare metrics before/after within the iteration window by logging
    baseline estimates via short interviews or existing manual records.
\end{itemize}

% 7. SCALABILITY
% ============================================================
\section{Scalability\secann{...with foresight}}

The proposition should scale in both theoretical and practical senses.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scalable (theoretically):} The system should support
    growth in student count and concurrent dashboard usage by:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item decoupling components (frontend/backend),
        \item enabling pagination and indexing for common queries,
        \item using stateless API design.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Operationally deployable (practically):} The system should
    run on common web hosting:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item managed database,
        \item containerized or platform-hosted deployment,
        \item CI/CD pipeline for staging/production.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Engine availability heuristic}

A mature set of ``engines'' (tooling/services) is available to implement this
as a web-based project:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Web framework for REST APIs and mobile-responsive pages.
    \item Managed relational or document database.
    \item Authentication approach (token-based or session-based).
    \item Object storage for optional menu images (or local storage for
    pilot).
    \item Test framework for unit/integration tests.
    \item CI runner and staging deployment target.
\end{itemize}

We will use these mature components to focus on billing correctness, workflow
design, and measurement rather than inventing new infrastructure.

% 10. RISKS AND MITIGATIONS
% ============================================================
\section{Risks and mitigations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Billing-model mismatch:} institution may be unwilling to
    move off a flat fee. \textit{Mitigation:} keep the billing model fully
    configurable per institution, with flat-fee mode as a fallback.
    \item \textbf{Unreliable participation signal:} students may forget to
    mark participation before the cutoff. \textit{Mitigation:} set sensible
    default cutoffs and consider opt-out-by-default where the institution
    prefers it.
    \item \textbf{Staff adoption:} mess staff may prefer existing
    paper/WhatsApp habits. \textit{Mitigation:} replace manual workflows one
    at a time in Phase 2 rather than all at once.
    \item \textbf{Connectivity in dining areas:} device/network access near
    hostel mess halls may be inconsistent. \textit{Mitigation:} design for
    degraded connectivity and confirm minimum device requirements with the
    pilot site.
    \item \textbf{Billing disputes:} students may forget to opt out and still
    be charged. \textit{Mitigation:} transparent, itemized billing view and
    clear cutoff communication.
\end{itemize}

% 11. SUMMARY
% ============================================================
\section{Summary}

This project proposes a deployable web platform that makes hostel mess billing
fairer and food preparation more efficient by connecting meal consumption to
both charges and demand planning. It emphasizes time-to-value through phased,
pilot-first delivery, uses CI/CD to support frequent safe releases, and
defines success with measurable, attributable evaluation criteria --- notably
consumption-to-billing latency. It remains focused on engineering workflow,
billing correctness, and scalability readiness.
